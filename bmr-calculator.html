About this tool

Your basal metabolic rate is the energy your body spends just staying alive, breathing, circulating blood, keeping warm, measured at complete rest. The Mifflin-St Jeor equation, published in 1990 and validated against measured metabolic rates since, predicts it from four numbers: for men, 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5; for women the same, ending in − 161 instead of + 5. Studies consistently find it the most accurate of the common formulas for non-obese adults, which is why dietitians reach for it first. BMR is a foundation, not a target: nobody eats at BMR, because nobody lives at rest, multiply it by activity to get your TDEE in the Calorie Calculator.

A note on health results: every figure this tool gives is an estimate from a standard formula, it is not medical advice and it does not diagnose anything. Bodies vary, and formulas cannot see your health history. Use the result as a starting point, and consult a clinician or registered dietitian for personal health decisions.

How to use it

  1. Pick your sex, the equation uses a different constant for men and women.
  2. Enter age, weight and height, kg/cm or lbs/ft-in.
  3. Read your BMR, per day, per week and per hour at rest.
  4. Go one step further, the Calorie Calculator multiplies it by your activity level.

What you get

  • Mifflin-St Jeor, the best-validated BMR equation in common use.
  • Both unit systems, kg/cm or lbs/ft-in.
  • Day, week and hour, your resting burn at three scales.
  • Formula shown, your own numbers substituted into the equation, so you can check the maths.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation?
Men: BMR = 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age in years + 5. Women: the same with − 161 at the end instead of + 5. It predicts resting calorie burn from body size and age, and research has found it more accurate than the older Harris-Benedict equation for most adults.
Should I eat exactly my BMR?
No. BMR assumes you lie still all day digesting nothing. Even a sedentary desk worker burns roughly 20% more than BMR just moving around and digesting food. Your real maintenance figure is your TDEE, BMR multiplied by an activity factor.
Why does BMR fall with age and weight loss?
The equation subtracts 5 kcal per year of age, reflecting the gradual loss of muscle mass as we get older. And a smaller body burns less: lose weight and your BMR drops too, which is one reason weight loss slows down over time.
