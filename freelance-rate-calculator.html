About this freelance rate calculator

Most freelancers set their rate by copying what others charge, then wonder why the month ends short. The rate that works is a floor built from your own numbers: the monthly income you want to keep, plus your business costs, grossed up for tax, divided by the hours you can actually bill. Most freelancers bill 15 to 25 hours a week, not 40, because the rest goes to finding clients, proposals and admin. This calculator uses that honest figure, plus the weeks you take off, to give the minimum hourly rate and the day rate for an 8-hour day. It is a floor, not a target: below it, your income goal cannot be reached even if every billable hour is sold. Add 20 to 30% above the floor to cover slow months.

How to use the freelance rate calculator

  1. Enter the income you want - the monthly amount you want to keep, after costs and tax.
  2. Add your monthly costs and tax - business costs like tools, data and transport, and one effective tax percent.
  3. Enter your real billable hours - hours per week clients actually pay for, and weeks off per year.
  4. Read your minimum rate - the hourly rate and day rate, plus what you must invoice each month and year.

What you get

  • Minimum hourly rate - the floor your rate must clear to hit your income goal.
  • Day rate included - the hourly rate times 8, for day-rate quotes.
  • Honest billable hours - weeks off and non-billable time are taken out first.
  • Monthly and yearly targets - what you must invoice each month and year to get there.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?
Add your target monthly income to your monthly business costs, gross the total up for tax, then divide by your billable hours. For example, ₦500,000 income plus ₦50,000 costs, at 10% tax, needs ₦611,111 invoiced a month. Over 1,200 billable hours a year, that is about ₦6,111 an hour. The Freelance Rate Calculator does the whole sum.
How many billable hours should I use?
Use the hours clients actually pay for, usually 15 to 25 a week, not 40. The rest of the week goes to finding clients, proposals, admin and learning. Using 40 billable hours sets a rate that is too low to live on.
Is the rate it gives me the rate I should charge?
It is the minimum: the floor below which your income goal cannot be reached. Most freelancers charge 20 to 30% above the floor, to cover slow months, unpaid invoices and time off. Your market rate and experience matter too.
